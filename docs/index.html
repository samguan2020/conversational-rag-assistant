How it works

Grounded answers, not just a chat window on top of an LLM

The core idea, from Lewis et al. (2020): instead of asking a model to answer from what's baked into its weights, retrieve the most relevant source text at query time and condition generation on it.

Ingest

A PDF/TXT upload is split into ~200-char chunks and embedded into that channel's vector store.

Retrieve

The question is embedded and matched by similarity against the same channel's vectors.

Generate

A ConversationalRetrievalChain condenses the question against history, then answers from retrieved context only.

Remember

A windowed memory (last 3 turns) keeps follow-up questions coherent without unbounded context growth.

Multi-tenancy

Channels: one deployment, many isolated knowledge bases

A channel is a separate vector-store namespace, selected by cookie. Uploading into one channel never leaks into another channel's retrieval results — the same service can back several independent topics or tenants.

Architecture

One factory module, two swappable backends

common/lc_modules.py is the only place that knows which LLM or vector store is in use. Routes and chat/ingestion logic call factory functions, not concrete providers — switching Gemini for GPT, or Redis for Cassandra, is a one-function change.

Engineering decisions

The small details that make RAG answers usable

Why a condense-question step

A follow-up like "what about the second one?" means nothing to a retriever on its own. The chain first rewrites the question against chat history into a standalone query, then retrieves — so follow-ups actually find the right chunks.

Why per-channel isolation

Each channel gets its own vector-store namespace/table. Without this, uploading a second topic's documents would silently pollute retrieval for the first.

Why a windowed memory, not full history

ConversationBufferWindowMemory(k=3) keeps only the last 3 turns. Long conversations don't blow up the prompt — recency is what matters most for resolving follow-ups.

Why one factory module for providers

lc_modules.py is the single seam between business logic and Gemini/GPT/Redis/Cassandra. Routes and services depend on the factory functions, never a concrete provider.
